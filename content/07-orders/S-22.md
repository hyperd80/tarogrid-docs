**Goal:** a repeatable play for every 🔴 order — one that filters fakes, keeps real customers, and never makes you the fraud police.

:::demo advance-shield · "The play, end to end" · 18s
Risky order → a small advance link, about the delivery fee → paid: confirms itself → unpaid: flagged for your review, never auto-cancelled.
:::

## The play

1. **Don't confirm it yet.** Red rows sit out the bulk-confirm ([S-16](#/S-16)).
2. **Look at the counts** — one tap on the light shows received vs returned ([F-21](#/F-21)). A thin history (new number) is a different story from five refusals.
3. **Send a friendly note first** if you like the human touch: *"Thanks for your order! Confirming your delivery for Thursday — shall we ship?"* A reply converts a 🟡-ish red into a confident yes.
4. **Request the advance** — the **Request advance** button on the row, or let the recipe do it automatically ([F-24](#/F-24)). Small amount, delivery-fee-sized, 24-hour link.
5. **Paid → it confirms itself** and joins the normal dispatch flow. **Expired → flagged for review** — your call: message again, ship anyway, or cancel.

## The tone that works

:::tip
Frame the advance as *confirming the delivery*, never as suspicion: "To lock in your delivery slot, here's a quick $5 confirmation — it comes off your total." Real buyers find it reasonable; fake orders were never going to pay anything anyway.
:::

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Don't cancel reds on sight — new phone numbers look red-ish and some are your best future customers ([the why, F-21](#/F-21)).
- ⚠️ Don't ship a red "because the order is big." Big is exactly where refuse-at-door hurts most — big orders deserve the advance *more*.

## Example

:::example
Monday's red: three dresses, $177, a number with four refusals on record. Sana sends the warm note plus the $5 link. Silence for a day; the link expires; the flag appears; she cancels without drama. Tuesday's red: new number, zero history — pays the advance in three minutes, ships same day, five-star customer since.
:::

**Goal:** know where every parcel is without opening a single courier portal — and catch the exceptions that need you.

:::demo track-deliveries · "Watching without watching" · 12s
Delivery statuses refresh themselves → a Returned parcel raises its hand and offers to restock.
:::

## How tracking works

Once an order is booked ([F-20](#/F-20)), TaroGrid checks with the courier and keeps the **Delivery Status** current on the row: *booked → in transit → delivered* (or *returned*). You don't refresh anything; the board simply tells the truth on its own schedule.

## What deserves your attention

- **Delivered (COD)** — good news, and a handoff: the cash is now the courier's debt to you, tracked in [Courier Cash](#/S-19).
- **Returned** — the exception that matters. TaroGrid notifies you and offers the **restock prompt** — one tap puts the product's stock back ([S-23](#/S-23) covers the full play, including the honest cost accounting).
- **Stuck in transit** — parcels sometimes sit. If a status hasn't moved in days, that's a call to your courier, not a TaroGrid mystery: the row shows exactly what the courier last said, and when.

:::tip
Group your Orders Board by Delivery Status for delivery-heavy days — the board becomes a live dispatch wall: what's moving, what's arrived, what came back.
:::

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Don't mark orders Delivered by hand to "tidy up" — the status is the courier's word, and Courier Cash builds receivables from it. Hand-tidying invents money.
- Don't panic-refresh — the watcher checks on its own; a status that hasn't changed means the courier hasn't said anything new.

## Example

:::example
Wednesday afternoon, Maya glances at the board grouped by delivery status: nine in transit, six delivered (all six already showing in Courier Cash as "to collect"), one returned — the restock prompt is waiting, and the scarf goes back on the shelf before dinner.
:::

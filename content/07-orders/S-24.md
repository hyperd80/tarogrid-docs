**Goal:** run the confirm-and-dispatch loop from your phone — at the market, in the stockroom, on the sofa.

:::demo phone-orders · "One hand, whole loop" · 10s
On your phone, orders become cards — everything important at a glance → swipe to confirm.
:::

## What changes on the phone

Rows become **cards**. Each order card leads with what a small screen should: customer, total, fraud light, status. Tap a card for the full order; the details are all there, arranged for a thumb instead of a mouse.

## The gestures

- **Swipe to confirm** — the daily decision ([S-16](#/S-16)), now a flick.
- **Tap the fraud light** for the counts before you swipe.
- Statuses, delivery tracking, and Courier Cash all read naturally — the phone shows the same truth as the desktop, dressed for the pocket.

:::note
It's the same workspace, not a satellite app — an order confirmed on your phone is confirmed everywhere before you've locked the screen ([how sync works](#/X-05)). Offline in a basement stockroom? Changes queue and flow when you're back ([W-06](#/W-06)).
:::

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Swipes are real confirms — the dispatch recipe doesn't know you were on the bus. Same meaning, same consequences as the desktop click ([S-16](#/S-16)).
- 💡 Phone-first sellers: put the shop's test order ([S-10](#/S-10)) through your own phone — you'll experience exactly what you'll manage.

## Example

:::example
Maya's Saturday market stall: between customers, she swipes through the morning's six online orders — five confirmed, booked by the recipe before the next customer walks up; one yellow saved for the quiet minute. The laptop stayed home.
:::

*With cash on delivery, the courier collects your money at the customer's door — and holds it until the next payout. Most sellers track that float in their head. This window is the end of that.*

:::demo courier-cash · "Follow the money home" · 22s
Where is my COD money? → per courier: to collect · paid to you · needs review → a payout arrives and matches your orders by tracking ID → an unmatched line waits for your eyes, never silently absorbed.
:::

## What the window answers, per courier

:::cards
- **To collect** — delivered orders whose cash the courier still holds. Your money, their pocket, on the record.
- **Paid to you** — reconciled: payout received and matched to specific orders.
- **Needs review** — lines that didn't match cleanly. Surfaced, never swallowed.
- **Last paid you** — when this courier last sent a payout. Silence past the usual rhythm is worth a call.
:::

## How money moves through it

1. An order goes **Delivered** with COD → its amount becomes a **receivable**: "to collect."
2. The courier pays out — you [log it yourself](#/S-21), or [fetch their statement](#/S-20) where supported.
3. TaroGrid **matches each payout line to your orders** — by tracking ID first, order ID second. Matched orders flip to **Reconciled** ([the last stop, S-15](#/S-15)).
4. It **never double-counts** — a line can't reconcile the same order twice — and it **never silently absorbs** a mismatch. What the courier claims is always checked against your own rows; disagreements land in **Needs review**.

:::why Why so strict about matching?
Because "the courier paid me something around the right amount" is how leaks hide. A missing $59 inside a $944 payout is invisible unless every line meets its order. A reconciled dollar is the only dollar that's real — everything else is a promise with your money in someone else's pocket.
:::

## Example

:::example
Maya opens Courier Cash on Friday: **to collect $118** (two delivered orders), **paid to you $944** this month, **needs review $59** — one payout line referenced a tracking ID she can't find. Two minutes of looking: the courier typo'd a digit; the line matches Anika's order. She resolves it, and the month's math closes clean. Before this window, that $59 would have quietly evaporated.
:::

## What to avoid

- ⚠️ Don't treat "delivered" as "paid" — that's the exact gap this window exists to watch ([S-15](#/S-15)).
- ⚠️ Don't clear Needs-review lines without looking — each one is either a typo (fixable) or missing money (yours).

## Best practices

- ✔ Reconcile on the courier's payout rhythm — weekly courier, weekly five-minute ritual.
- ✔ Watch "last paid you" — the earliest sign of payout trouble is a date that stopped moving.

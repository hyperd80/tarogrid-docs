**Goal:** handle a returned parcel in two honest moves: put the product back, and put the cost on the books.

:::demo returns-restock · "Two honest moves" · 12s
A parcel comes back → restock puts the product back on the shelf → and the return's real cost gets counted, not ignored.
:::

## Move 1 — restock

When a delivery flips to **Returned** ([S-18](#/S-18)), TaroGrid offers the **restock prompt**: one tap returns the product's stock. It's a prompt, not automatic — you confirm what actually came back intact. A crushed box with a damaged scarf isn't stock; it's a loss with packaging.

## Move 2 — count the cost

:::note
**The return-cost reality:** a refused COD parcel cost you the delivery fee out, often a return fee back, the packaging, and days of that product earning nothing. A $59 order refused at the door is not "$0, no harm" — it's typically $8–12 gone. Log it as an expense (the Money chapter shows the one-line way: [M-09](#/M-09)) so your net stays honest.
:::

Sellers who skip Move 2 have profitable-feeling months that end with less money than the board promised. The gap is almost always uncounted returns.

## Closing the loop

- The fraud light **learns**: this phone number's history now includes a return — the next order from it starts with honest context ([F-21](#/F-21)).
- If returns from one area or one product spike, that's a pattern worth a look — packaging? sizing? the courier's handling?

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Don't restock damaged goods to make the count look better — the shop will sell what you don't have in sellable condition.
- ⚠️ Don't delete returned orders "to clean the board." The row is history: it feeds the fraud light and explains your month.

## Example

:::example
The returned dress from Tuesday: box intact, dress fine — restocked, 11 → 12. Sana logs the $10 round-trip delivery as an expense against "returns." At month's end, Maya's net says exactly what happened: $10 of it went to a customer who changed their mind at the door. Annoying — but *counted*, which is the difference between a business and a guess.
:::

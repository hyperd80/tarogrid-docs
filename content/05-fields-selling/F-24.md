*The fourth selling superpower — and the answer to the oldest cash-on-delivery question: "how do I know they'll actually take the parcel?"*

:::demo advance-shield · "Skin in the game" · 18s
A risky order → a small advance link, about the delivery fee → paid: the order confirms itself → unpaid past expiry: flagged for your review, never auto-cancelled.
:::

## What is it?

A way to ask a customer for a **small advance** — typically about the delivery fee, say $5 — before you ship a risky cash-on-delivery order. They get a hosted payment link; the rest of the order stays COD.

A real buyer pays a small advance without blinking. A fake order never pays anything. That asymmetry is the whole shield.

## The lifecycle, in plain English

1. **Request** — tap **Request advance** on any order row, or let the recipe do it on 🔴 orders ([F-23](#/F-23)). The customer receives a payment link: small amount, 24-hour expiry.
2. **Paid** → the order moves to **Confirmed** — real skin in the game — and the dispatch chain takes over ([F-20](#/F-20)).
3. **Unpaid past expiry** → the order is **flagged for your review**. Your choice from there — message them, ship anyway, or cancel. The default outcome is deliberately kind: flag, never auto-cancel, because links get lost and people sleep.

Amounts, expiry, and the expiry outcome are editable per board.

## The money honesty box

:::note
The order never confirms until the payment provider itself verifies that real money landed — a screenshot of a payment is not a payment. A link can't be paid twice. An underpayment never counts as paid. These aren't features to configure; they're how it always works.
:::

⚠️ **One prerequisite:** advance links ride your connected payment gateway — set that up first (*Get paid online*, in the Money chapter). No gateway, no links — the button will tell you honestly.

## Why this instead of just cancelling reds?

:::why Filters beat verdicts
A red light says *risky*, not *fake* ([F-21](#/F-21)). Cancel every red and you cancel some real customers — new numbers, new neighborhoods, someone's first order. The advance converts exactly the real ones and costs exactly the fake ones nothing but silence. You stop guessing; the money decides.
:::

## Example

:::example
Two red orders on Maya's board on Monday. Both get the $5 advance link automatically. Order one: paid in four minutes — confirmed, booked, delivered Thursday; a first-time customer who's since ordered twice more. Order two: expired silently — flagged, Sana glances, cancels. Cost of the whole episode: zero shipped parcels gambled.
:::

## What to avoid

- ⚠️ Don't set the advance high "for safety." The point is a *trivial* amount for a real buyer — friction kills real orders faster than fraud does.
- Don't send advance requests to greens — the shield is for risk, not a toll booth for everyone.

## Best practices

- ✔ Keep the recipe's message warm — "to confirm your delivery" reads better than "to prove you're real."
- ✔ Review the flagged-and-expired pile weekly; it's your fraud radar *and* your reminder list, in one place.

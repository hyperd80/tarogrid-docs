*The third selling superpower: knowing, before the parcel leaves, whether it's likely to come back.*

:::demo fraud-light · "Green, yellow, red" · 16s
Every order gets a light → it reads the phone number's delivery history: received vs returned → a signal, not a verdict — red means check before you ship.
:::

## What is it?

A **traffic light for an order's risk** — green, yellow, or red — computed from the customer's *delivery track record*: how often parcels to this phone number were actually received versus refused or returned.

## Why it exists — the honest version

:::why The cost of a parcel that comes back
Fake orders and refuse-at-the-door are the tax on cash-on-delivery selling. A returned parcel costs you the delivery fee both ways, the packing, and the days your product spent traveling to nowhere. Knowing *before* you ship is real money — often the difference between a profitable month and a confusing one.
:::

## How it works

When an order lands (or on demand), TaroGrid checks the phone number's courier history and sets the light:

:::cards
- **🟢 Green** — this number receives its parcels. Ship without ceremony.
- **🟡 Yellow** — mixed record, or a new number with thin history. Worth a confirmation call.
- **🔴 Red** — high return risk. Check before you ship — or ask for an advance.
:::

The underlying counts — received vs returned — are one tap away. And on orders from your shop link, the light is **already resolved before you open the row** ([S-08](#/S-08)).

## A signal, not a verdict

This is the part the docs say plainly: the light reads *history*, and history can be thin. A new phone number isn't a criminal — it's unknown. Red means *check*, never *punish*.

## What to do with each light

Green flows untouched. Yellow gets a quick confirmation message. Red gets the headline play: **ask for a small advance** — about the delivery fee — via a payment link. A real buyer pays it in a minute; a fake order never does. That's [the advance shield, F-24](#/F-24) — the two fields are designed as a pair.

The automated version — *red ⇒ request advance automatically* — ships **off by default**; you turn it on knowingly ([the cookbook](#/F-23)).

## Example

:::example
Tomas's midnight Gift Bundle order arrives 🟡 — new number, no history. Sana sends the friendly confirmation message; he replies in the morning; she ships. The same week, an order for three dresses arrives 🔴 — that number refused four of its last five parcels. She sends the $5 advance link instead. It's never paid. Two delivery fees saved, zero customers offended.
:::

## What to avoid

- ⚠️ Don't treat 🔴 as a fact about a *person* — it's a pattern about a *number*, and numbers change hands.
- ⚠️ Don't blindly cancel reds. Request the advance instead — it converts the real buyers and filters the fake ones, automatically, politely.

## Best practices

- ✔ Let green flow — the light exists so you *stop* inspecting every order.
- ✔ Make the yellow-call a habit; it doubles as customer care.

*Module Windows are NOT the database. They are intelligent interfaces. This one idea explains half of TaroGrid.*

:::demo window-lifts · "The cockpit and the engine room" · 20s
The Invoice Center is open → it lifts like a sheet of glass, revealing the Invoice Board underneath → arrows flow both ways: the window reads and writes the board.
:::

## Overview

A **Module Window** is not where your data lives. It is an intelligent screen that works *on top of* one or more boards. The Invoice Center, Courier Cash, the Command Center — all windows. Underneath each one: ordinary boards, full of ordinary rows.

## What is it?

Think of a car. You drive using the cockpit — wheel, pedals, dashboard. You *could* open the hood and touch the engine, but for daily driving the cockpit is safer and faster.

The window is the *cockpit*; the board is the *engine room*. You can always open the engine room — the board — and see the raw rows a window is showing you. Nothing is hidden.

## Why it exists

:::why Why not just give me the boards?
Because an invoice is more than a row. It needs a number that's never reused, a document your customer can open, a paid/unpaid life of its own. A raw board can hold the *data*, but the *rules* need a smarter screen. The window carries the rules; the board carries the truth. You get both: friendly on top, honest underneath.
:::

## The four kinds of windows

:::cards
- **Summary** — your whole business at a glance. *Example: the Command Center.*
- **Dashboard** — charts and numbers over your data. *Example: billing overview, Insights.*
- **Control center** — runs one area end to end. *Examples: Invoice Center · Receipts · Expenses · Courier Cash · Stakeholders.*
- **Workflow** — walks a job through steps. *Examples: the Sales Form builder · the order confirm flow · ASK's order card.*
:::

## How windows and boards pair up

One window can read **many** boards: the Command Center reads Invoices + Receipts + Expenses + Orders to compute your net.

One board can feed **many** windows: the Orders Board powers the order funnel, Courier Cash, *and* the Command Center — same rows, three cockpits.

## Where you'll use it — and when

Daily work happens in windows: invoicing in the Invoice Center, cash-on-delivery money in Courier Cash. Open the board underneath when you want the raw rows — to scan everything at once, filter, or check history.

## Example

:::example
**Maya checks a payment.** In the **Invoice Center** she sees "Anika — $59 — Paid" as a tidy card. Curious, she opens the Invoice Board underneath: there's the same invoice as a row, with its history — created Tuesday, published Wednesday, paid Friday, each with who and when. Same fact, two altitudes. She closes the board and goes back to the cockpit.
:::

## Relationship with other features

- Every window keeps its data on a **locked system board** — the next article, [Built-in system boards and the lock](#/C-04), is this idea's other half.
- **ASK** ([A tour of the screen](#/W-04)) opens any window by name — ⌘K, three letters, Enter.
- **Permissions** ([C-07](#/C-07)) decide who can open which window at all.

## What to avoid

- ⚠️ Don't edit a window's board directly to "fix" what the window shows — the window will faithfully rewrite it. Fix things *in* the window; that's the writer.
- Don't hunt for a "hidden database." There isn't one. Lift any window and you'll find a board you can read.

## Best practices

- ✔ Do daily work in the window; visit the board to *understand*, filter, or audit.
- ✔ When a number surprises you, lift the hood: open the board and read the rows behind it.

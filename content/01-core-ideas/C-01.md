*A Workspace is a company. Learn what lives inside the wall — and why the wall matters.*

:::demo workspace-walls · "Two companies, one screen, zero mixing" · 18s
Two workspaces sit side by side → an order lands in one; the other doesn't blink → switching flips everything: boards, money, people.
:::

## Overview

A **Workspace** is a company. Everything inside it belongs to that company — its information, its money records, its people, its work. If you run two separate businesses, you give each its own Workspace, and they never mix.

## What is it?

Think of a Workspace as your shop building. Inside are your ledgers (boards), your cash records (Money), your address book and staff (People), and your workbench (Work). When you invite a teammate, you're letting them into *this building* — not into every business you own.

## Why it exists

:::why Why draw a hard wall around a company?
Because mixing two businesses' money and customers is how confusion — and worse, wrong numbers — happen. One Workspace means one truthful answer to "how is *this* business doing?" It also means you can give your team the keys to one business without exposing another.
:::

## How it works

- Everything you create — boards, orders, invoices, automations — lives inside exactly one Workspace.
- Your **team** is per-workspace: Sana can be in Maya's Closet without ever seeing Atlas Studio.
- Switching Workspaces switches *everything*: sidebar, boards, money, people. Nothing follows you across the wall.

:::

## Where you'll use it — and when

You'll *live* inside one Workspace; most people only ever need one. Reach for a second Workspace only when you genuinely run a second business — separate money, separate customers, separate team.

## Common use cases

- One seller, one shop → one Workspace.
- A seller who also runs a design studio → two Workspaces.
- An agency managing its own operations → one Workspace (client work lives on boards *inside* it).

## Example

:::example
**Maya's cousin problem.** Maya's cousin runs Atlas Studio and wants "to share the app." They don't share a Workspace — the studio gets its own. Maya's $59 orders never appear in the studio's money, and the studio's client list never mixes with Maya's customers. When the cousin needs Maya's help, he invites her into *his* Workspace as a member.
:::

## Relationship with other features

- **Boards** ([C-02](#/C-02)) live inside a Workspace — every last one.
- **The four categories** ([C-05](#/C-05)) — Money, People, Work, Outreach — are how a Workspace organizes itself.
- **Your team** joins per-workspace; **permissions** ([C-07](#/C-07)) decide what each person sees inside it.

## What to avoid

- ⚠️ Don't create a Workspace per project, per month, or per product line. Those belong on boards *inside* your Workspace. A second Workspace is a second *company* — with its own separate everything.
- Don't run two businesses inside one Workspace "for convenience." The day you hire help for one of them, you'll wish the wall existed.

## Best practices

- ✔ Name the Workspace what the business is called — it appears on your shop and your documents.
- ✔ One business, one Workspace. Truly separate business? Then a second one.

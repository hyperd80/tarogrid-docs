## Overview

A board is where TaroGrid stores one kind of information — orders, products, customers, tasks. It looks like a smart table. Each **row** is one thing. Each **column** is one fact about that thing.

## What is it?

Think of the notebook a shopkeeper keeps: one line per order, with the customer's name, what they bought, and whether they paid.

A board is that notebook, made intelligent. It never runs out of pages. It can sort itself, count itself, warn you, and even do work for you.

## Why it exists

:::why Why boards?
Your business is really a collection of *lists that must not lie*: orders, products, people, money. Paper lists get lost. Chat messages scroll away. Spreadsheets let anyone break a formula. A board keeps each list safe, structured, and connected to the rest of your business — so one true copy exists, and everything else reads from it.
:::

Every cell quietly remembers its history — what it said before, who changed it, and when. That's why you can always undo, and always answer "who changed this?"

Some boards **you create** — any shape you like (see *Work — your canvas*).

Some boards are **system boards** — TaroGrid builds and maintains them to power Module Windows like the Invoice Center. System boards are **locked**, because the window writes to them; your job is to use the window, and to add *your own* columns if you want more. (Full story: [Built-in system boards — and the lock](#/C-04).)

## Where you'll use it — and when

Everywhere. Your shop's orders land on the **Orders Board**. Your products live behind the **Catalog**. Your customers behind **Stakeholders**. Your invoices behind the **Invoice Center**. And in **Work**, you'll create your own boards for anything you run.

Reach for a board whenever you catch yourself keeping a list anywhere else — a notebook, a chat with yourself, a spreadsheet.

## Common use cases

- Tracking every order from *Pending* to *Delivered*.
- A product shelf with prices and stock.
- A simple CRM: leads and where each one stands.
- A task list for you and your team.
- A content calendar for your social pages.

## Example

:::example
**Maya's Closet.** Maya's Orders Board has three groups: **New**, **Shipped**, **Done**. Each row is one order. Columns: Customer · Phone · Products · Total · Fraud · Status · Courier. When Anika orders a Rose Dress from the shop link, a new row appears in **New** by itself — Maya never typed it. When Sana books the courier, the row slides to **Shipped**. Maya's whole day is: read the board top to bottom.
:::

## Relationship with other features

- **Views** are glasses for the same board — Table, Kanban, Calendar ([Views — same data, different glasses](#/B-03)).
- **Fields** define what each column can hold (*What is a field?*, coming in the Fields chapter).
- **Module Windows** are cockpits that read and write boards ([Module Windows](#/C-03)).
- **Automations** watch boards and act ([Automations](#/AU-01)).
- **ASK** can find rows, answer questions about them, and create them ([Meet ASK](#/A-01)).

## What to avoid

- ⚠️ Don't keep two boards for the same list "to be safe." Two copies always drift apart. One board, one truth.
- ⚠️ Don't fight a locked column on a system board — the module will rewrite it. Add your *own* column instead.
- Don't make one giant column called "Details" — one fact per column is what makes sorting, filtering, and automations possible.

## Best practices

- ✔ Name boards for what a row *is*: "Orders," "Products," "Leads" — not "Maya's stuff."
- ✔ Start with 4–6 columns. Add more when a real need appears.
- ✔ Use groups for *stages or sections you'd say out loud* ("New / Shipped / Done").
- ✔ Let forms, ASK, and modules write rows for you whenever possible — typed-by-hand is where typos live.

*You typed a value. It looked saved. Later, it's back to what it was. Here's what actually happened — and the two-minute fix.*

## What happened

The cell you edited is on a **built-in board**, in a column a Module Window owns — the Invoice Center, the shop, Campaigns, Deals, the Catalog ([who writes what, C-04](#/C-04)). The window is that column's writer: on its next refresh, it wrote its own value back over yours.

Nothing stopped you at the time, and nothing warned you afterwards. That's why this looks like a ghost. It isn't: **two writers on one cell means the numbers stop being trustworthy**, so the module always wins its own columns.

:::tip How to tell before it happens again
On a built-in board, a **grey** column heading means the column came built in — usually the module's. A **bold** heading means you added it, and it's yours forever. Full rule in [C-04](#/C-04).
:::

## The fix, by what you were trying to do

:::cards
- **Fixing wrong data** (a price, an amount, a status) → make the change **in the window that owns it** — the Invoice Center for invoice amounts ([M-02](#/M-02)), the Catalog for prices ([S-11](#/S-11)). The board follows the writer, so fix the writer's source.
- **Adding your own information** (a note, a tag, a photo reference) → **add your own column** on that board. No unlocking needed, and your columns survive every refresh, forever ([C-04](#/C-04)'s Maya example is exactly this).
- **Disagreeing with what the module computed** (a fraud light, a total) → the value is derived; overtyping it can't change the inputs. Check what feeds it — [F-21](#/F-21) for the light, the Catalog for totals.
- **It keeps happening to a teammate** → lock the board: **Board settings → Lock board** makes it read-only for everyone until it's unlocked.
:::

## The aha worth keeping

:::why This "bug" is the system keeping its promise
A window and its board are cockpit and engine room ([C-03](#/C-03)) — and every gauge needs exactly one writer. The day your edit *did* stick on a module's column would be the day the Invoice Center's totals could silently disagree with the invoices. The revert you saw is the reason you never have to wonder which number is real.
:::

One sentence to leave with: **extend the board, never fight the module** ([C-04](#/C-04)).

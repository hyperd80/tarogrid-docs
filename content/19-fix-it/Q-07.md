*You typed a value. It looked saved. Later, it's back to what it was. Here's what actually happened — and the two-minute fix.*

## What happened

The cell you edited is a **module-managed column** on a system board — a column that a Module Window (the Invoice Center, the shop, Campaigns) considers its own to write ([the lock, C-04](#/C-04)). The window is the writer: on its next refresh, it rewrote its column — including over your edit.

Your edit wasn't rejected out of spite; it was overwritten by design, because two writers on one cell means the numbers stop being trustworthy.

## The fix, by what you were trying to do

:::cards
- **Fixing wrong data** (a price, an amount, a status) → make the change **in the window that owns it** — the Invoice Center for invoice amounts ([M-02](#/M-02)), the Catalog for prices ([S-11](#/S-11)). The board follows the writer, so fix the writer's source.
- **Adding your own information** (a note, a tag, an outcome) → unlock the board and **add your own column** — user columns survive every refresh, forever ([C-04](#/C-04)'s Maya example is exactly this).
- **Disagreeing with what the module computed** (a fraud light, a total) → the value is derived; overtyping it can't change the inputs. Check what feeds it — [F-21](#/F-21) for the light, the Catalog for totals.
:::

## The aha worth keeping

:::why This "bug" is the system keeping its promise
A window and its board are cockpit and engine room ([C-03](#/C-03)) — and every gauge needs exactly one writer. The day your edit *did* stick on a module column would be the day the Invoice Center's totals could silently disagree with the invoices. The revert you saw is the reason you never have to wonder which number is real.
:::

One sentence to leave with: **unlock to extend, never to fight the module** ([C-04](#/C-04)).

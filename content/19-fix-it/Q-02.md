*A customer says they ordered; your board looks empty. Run these checks top to bottom — most people stop at the second one.*

## The checks, most-likely first

1. **Filter chips.** Look above the board: any chips? A *Status: Confirmed* filter hides every new Pending row. Clear the chips ([B-08](#/B-08)) — this is the answer half the time.
2. **The right group, the right view.** New orders land in *Pending* — scroll to that group; on a Kanban, check that column. Sorting can also push the newest row somewhere unexpected — sort by newest first.
3. **The right workspace.** Two businesses, two workspaces ([C-01](#/C-01)) — are you in the one whose shop was ordered from?
4. **Did they finish?** "I ordered" sometimes means "I almost ordered." Ask what the last screen said — no confirmation screen, no order. Check whether they hit an out-of-stock wall ([S-13](#/S-13)).
5. **Is the form live?** An unpublished or paused form takes no orders ([S-13](#/S-13)). Open your own link fresh and look.
6. **The connection.** Orders land in the cloud, then flow to your device ([W-06](#/W-06)). Offline devices show the past — reconnect and let the queue drain.

## Prove the pipe in two minutes

Still nothing? Place a **test order yourself** ([the rehearsal, S-10](#/S-10)). Your test appears → the pipe works, and the customer's order never completed. Your test doesn't appear → now it's real; note what the form's last screen said and contact support with that.

:::note
In practice, a *completed* order has not once been "lost between the form and the board" — the form writes the row directly ([S-08](#/S-08)); there's no in-between place to lose it. The mystery is always visibility (filters, groups, workspace) or completion (they didn't finish).
:::

## Prevent the next one

Keep a saved unfiltered view — chips-free glasses you can always put on. And run the [pre-launch test](#/S-10) after any form change.

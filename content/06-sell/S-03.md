## Variants — one product, many options

1. Open the product in the **Catalog**.
2. Add options: *Size — S / M / L*, *Color — rose / cream*.
3. Give each option its own stock count if quantities differ.

Your shop now shows one product with choice chips — not three near-duplicate products. Orders record exactly which option was bought, and that option's stock counts down.

## Sale price — set it and stop thinking

1. On the product, set **Sale price** below the regular price.
2. That's all. The shop shows the sale price (old price struck through), order totals use it, invoices use it.

When the sale ends, clear the sale price — everything returns to normal the same instant.

:::tip
A sale price is a *second* price, not an edit of the first. Your real price is never lost, so ending a sale is one click, not an archaeology dig.
:::

## Stock tracking — or not

- **Tracked:** enter a count. Every sale counts it down; at zero, the shop shows the product as unavailable instead of overselling.
- **Untracked:** for services, pre-orders, made-to-order — nothing counts, nothing blocks.

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Don't run a sale by overwriting the regular price — you'll forget the original. Use the sale price field; that's its whole job.
- ⚠️ If two options share one pile of physical stock, give them one shared count — two counts for one pile oversells.

## Example

:::example
Maya's Rose Dress comes in S, M, L: stock 4, 8, and 0. On the shop, L shows as unavailable by itself. Holiday sale: sale price $49 — every surface flips at once. January: she clears it, and $59 is back everywhere.
:::

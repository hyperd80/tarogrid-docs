*TaroGrid has two kinds of public forms. Confusing them is harmless — but knowing the difference makes both click.*

:::demo two-doors · "Two doors, same trick" · 18s
The Sales Form: a door for customers — products, payment, delivery → an intake form: a door for requests, straight onto a Work board → forms write rows.
:::

:::figure golden-path
:::

## The two doors

:::cards
- **🛍️ The Sales Form** — built for *selling*. It carries your Catalog (real products, live prices, stock), takes payment choices, collects courier-ready fields, and writes to the **Orders Board** — where fraud checks, stock, couriers, and money all connect.
- **📋 A classic intake form** — built for *collecting*. You design its questions freely, attach it to any board **you** made in Work, and every submission becomes a row there. Client requests, event signups, repair jobs — anything.
:::

**The shared trick:** both are doors into a board. A form is never a separate pile of submissions to re-copy — the submission *is* the row ([the same lesson as S-08](#/S-08)).

## How to choose in one question

**Is money changing hands for products?** → Sales Form. **Anything else?** → intake form on a Work board.

:::why Why not one form type for everything?
Because selling carries machinery collecting doesn't need — stock, prices, payments, fraud, couriers. Bolting that onto every feedback form would make simple things heavy. Two doors, each light for its own job.
:::

## Example

:::example
Maya uses her **Sales Form** for the shop. When she started offering custom embroidery, she added an **intake form** — "Tell me about your piece" — attached to a *Custom Requests* board in Work: name, phone, description, photo. Requests arrive as rows; she quotes each one personally, and the ones that become orders go through the shop like everything else.
:::

## Pitfalls

- Don't sell through an intake form "because it's simpler" — you'd be re-typing products, prices, and totals by hand, which is every error the Sales Form exists to prevent.
- Don't collect job applications through your Sales Form — no, nobody's done it yet. Keep it that way.

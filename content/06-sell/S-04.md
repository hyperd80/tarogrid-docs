**Goal:** sell a set — like a dress and scarf together — at its own price, without your stock counts drifting from reality.

:::demo combo-bundle · "One bundle, two stocks" · 15s
Two products combine into a Gift Bundle with its own price → one bundle sale draws down BOTH member stocks.
:::

:::figure golden-path
:::

## Build a bundle

1. In the **Catalog**, create a combo and pick its member products — say, the Rose Dress and the Silk Scarf.
2. Give it a name, a photo, and its own price: *Gift Bundle — $69* (bought separately: $78).
3. Publish. The bundle appears on your shop like any product.

## The part that keeps you honest

When a customer buys one Gift Bundle, TaroGrid draws down **each member's stock**: one dress *and* one scarf. The bundle never has phantom stock of its own — it can only be ordered while every member is actually in stock.

:::why Why draw down the members instead of counting bundles?
Because the bundle isn't a thing on your shelf — the dress and scarf are. If bundles had their own count, you could "have" five bundles while the last dress sold out separately. Counting members means the shop can never promise a set you can't pack.
:::

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Don't also create the bundle as a separate normal product "so it has stock." That's the drift the combo exists to prevent.
- 💡 A bundle's price is independent — a member's sale price doesn't change the bundle. Decide bundle pricing deliberately.

## Example

:::example
Maya's *Gift Bundle* sells four times during the holidays. Dresses: 12 → 8. Scarves: 30 → 26. When dresses hit zero, the bundle goes unavailable on the shop by itself — even though scarves remain. No oversold sets, no apology messages.
:::

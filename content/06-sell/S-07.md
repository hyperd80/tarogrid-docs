**Goal:** make everything a customer sees — every form, every page — recognizably *yours*, from one setting.

:::demo shop-branding · "Inherit, then override" · 14s
The shop's look is set once → every form inherits it → one form overrides just its background, "this form only."
:::

:::figure golden-path
:::

## How it works: inherit first

Your **shop identity** — name, logo, accent color, look — is set once, at the shop level. Every Sales Form **inherits** it automatically. Change the accent color today, and every form re-paints. No form-by-form repetition, no forgotten stragglers.

## Override second — per piece, per form

Sometimes one form needs to differ: a holiday landing form, a wholesale form with a calmer look. Open that form's branding and flip any single piece to **"this form only"** — background, accent, whatever. Everything you *don't* override keeps following the shop.

:::why Why inherit-then-override instead of per-form settings?
Because brands drift one exception at a time. With inheritance, your look has one home; exceptions are explicit, visible, and reversible. The day you re-brand, one change moves everything — except the pieces you deliberately pinned.
:::

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ After a re-brand, remember chat apps cache old link previews ([S-06](#/S-06)) — the live page is current even when a stale preview says otherwise.
- Don't override every piece on every form "to be safe" — you'd be turning off inheritance and signing up to maintain each form by hand forever.

## Example

:::example
Maya sets her look once: rose accent, her logo, soft cream background. Her main form and her holiday form both inherit it. For the holidays she overrides one thing on the holiday form — a festive background, *this form only*. In January she removes the override; the form falls back to the shop look by itself.
:::

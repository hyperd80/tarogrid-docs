Most software teaches you buttons. Buttons change; ideas don't.

TaroGrid runs on exactly three ideas. Learn them once, and every screen you ever meet will make sense — even screens this Help Center hasn't written about yet.

:::demo three-ideas · "Company → databases → windows" · 20s
The camera starts on your company → zooms into a board of rows → a window slides on top → the window lifts, showing the board underneath.
:::

## Idea 1 — Your Workspace is your company

A Workspace is a company. Everything inside it belongs to that company — its information, its money records, its people, its work. If you run two separate businesses, you give each its own Workspace, and they never mix.

Think of a Workspace as the shop building. Everything in this Help Center happens inside it.

:::figure workspace-map
:::

## Idea 2 — Boards are your company's databases

A **board** is a database — think of it as a smart ledger book or filing cabinet. Every board stores one kind of information as rows. Each row is one thing (one order, one customer, one product). Each column is one fact about that thing (name, phone, price, status).

Some boards **you create** — your own ledgers, any shape you want.

Some boards are **system boards** that TaroGrid creates and maintains for you: the Invoice Board, the Receipt Board, the Orders Board, the Stakeholders Board. System boards power the Module Windows — that's Idea 3.

:::note
"Database" just means *an organized place where information lives*. Nothing technical. A paper ledger was already a database; a board is a ledger that can count, sort, warn, and work for you.
:::

## Idea 3 — Module Windows are intelligent screens, not the database

A **Module Window** is not where your data lives. It is an intelligent screen that works *on top of* one or more boards. Some windows are **summaries** (the Cockpit), some are **dashboards** (billing overview), some are **control centers** (Invoice Center, Courier Cash), and some are **workflows** (the Sales Form builder).

The window is the *cockpit*; the board is the *engine room*. You can always open the engine room — the board — and see the raw rows the window is showing you. Nothing is hidden.

:::figure window-board
:::

One more thing belongs to this idea: on a system board, the window owns the columns it writes. You can type anywhere — but a module rewrites its own columns on the next refresh. There's a whole article on it — [Built-in system boards — who writes what](#/C-04). For now, remember one sentence: *the window is the writer; add your own columns, don't fight the module's.*

## The three ideas, walked through once

🛍️ **Maya's Closet.** Anika orders a Rose Dress for $59.

- **Idea 1:** it all happens inside the *Maya's Closet* Workspace — her company. Her cousin's design studio, in a different Workspace, sees none of it.
- **Idea 2:** the order becomes one row on the **Orders Board**. Customer: Anika. Phone: her number. Total: $59. Status: Pending. One row, one order — each column one fact.
- **Idea 3:** when Maya invoices Anika, she works in the **Invoice Center** — a friendly cockpit. The invoice itself is stored as a row on the Invoice Board, the engine room. If Maya ever wants to see all invoices as raw rows, she opens that board and there they are.

## What to avoid

- ⚠️ Don't create a second Workspace for a project or a new product line. A Workspace is a whole company. Projects and product lines are boards *inside* your one Workspace.
- Don't hand-edit a module's column on a system board. Nothing stops you, but the window that manages it will rewrite it. This is not a bug — it's [Idea 3 doing its job](#/C-04).

## Best practices

- ✔ When a screen confuses you, ask: *am I looking at a board (the data) or a window (a smart screen on top of data)?* The answer usually clears the fog.
- ✔ One business = one Workspace. Two businesses = two Workspaces.
- ✔ Let windows do the writing where they can — hand-typing is where typos live.

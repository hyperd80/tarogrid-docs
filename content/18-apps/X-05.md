*You've seen the promise — device copy + cloud copy, always catching up ([W-06](#/W-06)). This is the article for the one interesting question: what if two people edit the same thing?*

:::demo sync-conflict · "The pick-one dialog" · 16s
Two devices, offline, edit the same cell → TaroGrid never silently picks a winner — it asks you → the version you don't pick stays in history.
:::

## The normal case: no conflict at all

You edit the status; Sana edits the address — **different facts, both win**, even on the same row at the same moment. Because boards are one-fact-per-cell ([C-06](#/C-06)), almost all simultaneous work simply interleaves. Days of teamwork pass without a single collision.

## The rare case: same cell, both offline

You set the total to $59 on the desktop; Sana sets it to $54 on her phone in the same offline window. Two claims, one cell.

**TaroGrid refuses to guess.** When the copies meet, it shows the **pick-one dialog**: both versions, who made each, when. You choose. The unpicked version isn't destroyed — it stays in the **cell's history**, forever recoverable ([B-11](#/B-11)).

:::why Why ask me instead of "latest wins"?
Because "latest" is an accident of whose wifi reconnected first — not who was *right*. A silent rule would quietly overwrite a correct number with a stale one and tell no one. The dialog costs you five seconds; the silent alternative costs you trust in every number on the board.
:::

## What you'll actually experience

- Online, same board, live: you see each other's edits appear — presence and edits flow in real time. Conflicts essentially can't happen while both copies talk.
- The dialog appears only after *offline* same-cell edits meet — for most teams, a few times a year. It's a surprise precisely because everything else is silent and correct.

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Don't resolve a pick-one dialog on autopilot — it appears exactly because both versions were deliberate. Read the who-and-when.
- 💡 See the dialog *often*? Two people co-own one cell's job — split the responsibility ([one writer per fact](#/C-04), the human edition).

## Example

:::example
The stockroom has no signal. Maya (desk) fixes a total to $59; Sana (basement) sets $54 from the customer's revised chat. Sana surfaces, the copies meet, the dialog shows both. $54 was the newer agreement — Maya picks it, the $59 stays in history with her name, and nobody overwrote anybody in silence.
:::

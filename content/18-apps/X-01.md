*It's the same workspace everywhere. The only question is which door fits the moment.*

:::demo three-frames · "Three doors, one home" · 12s
The same workspace on web, desktop, and phone → an edit on one flows to the others, usually before you look up.
:::

## The three doors

:::cards
- **The web app** — any browser, any computer, sign in and you're home. Zero installation; ideal for a borrowed laptop or a quick check. (On shared computers: sign out — it clears the local copy, [W-06](#/W-06).)
- **The desktop app** — installed, always-ready, **offline-first**: the full local copy means the app opens instantly and works through any outage. The daily driver for desk work.
- **Your phone** — the pocket door: order cards, swipe-to-confirm ([S-24](#/S-24)), the whole truth dressed for a thumb. Where your business goes when you do.
:::

## One workspace, honestly

The doors differ in *ergonomics*, not *truth*. An order confirmed on the phone is confirmed on the desktop; a price edited on the web is the price everywhere — [sync](#/X-05) keeps every copy in step, and offline edits queue and flow when the connection returns ([W-06](#/W-06)).

:::tip
Match the door to the moment: desktop for the morning dispatch run, phone for the market stall and the sofa, web for anywhere that isn't yours. No door is the "lite" version.
:::

## Example

:::example
Maya's Tuesday: desktop for [confirm & dispatch](#/S-16) at 10, phone at the fabric market at noon (two orders confirmed in a queue), web on the studio's shared computer at 4 (signed out after). One workspace the whole day — it just kept changing clothes.
:::

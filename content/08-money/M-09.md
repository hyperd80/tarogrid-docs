*Money in gets documents. Money out gets forgotten — unless logging it costs less than forgetting it. Here, it's one sentence.*

:::demo expense-ask · "One sentence, on the books" · 14s
⌘K: “spent 20 on boost” → a confirm card — nothing happens until you agree → logged, and your net already knows.
:::

## The fastest way: type it

Press **⌘K** and type what happened, the way you'd say it:

- *"spent 20 on boost"* → Ads, $20, today
- *"spent 8 on packaging tape"* → Packaging, $8
- *"paid courier 45 for pickups"* → Courier fees, $45

ASK shows a **confirm card** — what it understood, as chips you can correct — and logs the expense when you agree ([how ASK's safety works](#/A-01)).

## The Expenses window

Behind the sentences: the **Expenses window**, with friendly categories — ads, packaging, software, contractors, fees, equipment, travel, other. Like every money surface, it rides a **locked system board** ([C-04](#/C-04)): the window writes; your own extra columns are welcome; the math is protected.

## The payoff

:::why Why log a $3 expense?
Not for the $3 — for the **net**. Money in minus money out is the only number that says whether the month worked, and it's only as honest as its worst-tracked side. Sellers track income to the cent and expenses by vibe; the vibe is why profitable-looking months end with empty pockets. One sentence per expense buys you a real answer ([M-01](#/M-01)).
:::

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Don't batch "I'll log the month later" — later's memory is fiction with confidence. The sentence takes six seconds *now*.
- 💡 Return costs count ([S-23](#/S-23)). Gateway fees count. Small and invisible is exactly the money that leaks.

## Example

:::example
Maya's Tuesday, as typed: *spent 20 on boost · spent 8 on tissue paper · paid courier 45.* Eighteen seconds total. Month-end, her Cockpit net is a number she'd defend to an accountant — because every last dollar of spending is on the record.
:::

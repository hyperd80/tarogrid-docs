*The Money category has four documents and one number. Learn how they chain, and no money screen will ever surprise you.*

## Money in — the document chain

- **An order** says *someone owes you* ([the selling chapters](#/S-01)).
- **An invoice** makes it formal: a numbered document your customer can hold you to ([M-02](#/M-02), [M-04](#/M-04)).
- **A payment** is money actually arriving — online through your gateway ([M-08](#/M-08)), or cash on delivery through [Courier Cash](#/S-19).
- **A receipt** is proof it arrived — and it **mints itself** when a payment lands. You never write receipts; paid things simply have them.

Each step creates the next; nothing is re-typed. That's why the amounts can never disagree.

## Money out — expenses

Everything you spend — ads, packaging, courier fees, that returned parcel ([S-23](#/S-23)) — logged in seconds, mostly by [one typed sentence](#/M-09).

## The number

:::why Why does TaroGrid obsess over the chain?
Because "did I make money this month?" is unanswerable when orders live in chat, payments in a wallet app, and costs in your head. With every dollar in and out on the record, **net = money in − money out** becomes a number on your screen instead of a feeling in your stomach — and the feeling is wrong more often than sellers like to know.
:::

## Where it all lives

Every money document sits on a locked system board under its window ([the lock, C-04](#/C-04)) — friendly cockpits on top, auditable rows underneath, and nobody can fat-finger an invoice number.

## Example

:::example
Anika's $59 order → invoice INV-2026-014 → paid online Friday → receipt minted itself the same second. Meanwhile the month's expenses: $86 logged in one-line entries. Cockpit says **net +$486**. Maya knows — not hopes, knows.
:::

*An invoice's status isn't decoration — each state means one specific, verifiable thing.*

:::demo invoice-life · "An honest public life" · 16s
Draft → Sent → Viewed → Paid, each stamping itself → and Overdue gets a reminder recipe, so you don't have to be the nag.
:::

## The states, precisely

:::cards
- **Draft** — yours alone. No number, no obligations, edit freely ([M-02](#/M-02)).
- **Sent** — published and delivered a link ([M-04](#/M-04)). The clock on your payment terms starts.
- **Viewed** — the public page was actually opened. Quietly ends the "did you get it?" era.
- **Paid** — money verified. Stamped by the system when the gateway confirms ([M-08](#/M-08)) or when you record a payment you received directly — and the receipt mints itself ([M-01](#/M-01)).
- **Overdue** — past the due date, unpaid. Not shameful, just true — and actionable.
:::

## The overdue recipe

Turn on *Invoice overdue ⇒ remind* and TaroGrid sends a polite nudge with the invoice link when the due date passes — once, honestly, visibly logged ([how recipes behave](#/F-23)).

:::why Why let a robot send the reminder?
Because you'll postpone it. Everyone does — reminding feels like conflict, so unpaid invoices age in silence, and old invoices are the hardest to collect. The robot has no feelings on the matter: day one overdue, friendly note, every time. Your cash flow stops depending on your appetite for awkwardness.
:::

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ *Paid* means verified money, not a promise of money. "I sent it, check tomorrow" stays Overdue until it isn't — the status is a fact, not a mood.
- 💡 A pile of *Sent, never Viewed* invoices is a delivery problem (wrong chat, wrong person), not a payment problem. The badge tells you which fight you're in.

## Example

:::example
Maya's month-end glance at the Invoice Center: 12 Paid, 2 Sent-and-Viewed (terms not due yet), 1 Overdue — the recipe nudged it this morning; it was paid by lunch. Total collection effort spent by a human: zero.
:::

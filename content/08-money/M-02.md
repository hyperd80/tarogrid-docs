**Goal:** a complete, correct invoice in two minutes — and the confidence that a draft can't hurt anything.

:::demo first-invoice · "Two minutes, no fear" · 14s
Line items: product, quantity, price → the total builds itself → it's a draft: no number yet, edit freely.
:::

## The steps

1. Open the **Invoice Center** (⌘K → "inv") and create an invoice.
2. **Pick the customer.** An existing customer brings their details; a new one needs a phone or email — same identity rule as everywhere ([S-08](#/S-08)).
3. **Add line items.** Pick products from your Catalog — they price themselves, sale price winning ([F-19](#/F-19)) — or type free lines for services. Drag lines to reorder; edit inline.
4. Watch the **total build itself**: subtotal, any tax terms you've set, the number your customer will see.
5. Stop whenever you like. It's a **draft** — visible to you only, carrying **no invoice number**, editable forever.

**Result:** a document one click from publishable — which is the moment it becomes real ([M-04](#/M-04)).

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Drafts have no number *on purpose* — numbers are minted at publish so your sequence has no gaps from abandoned drafts. A draft claiming a number would be a small lie with audit consequences.
- 💡 Invoicing an order? Start from the order row — the line items carry over; re-typing is where totals drift.

## Example

:::example
Maya invoices a boutique's wholesale order: 6 × Rose Dress from the Catalog (prices itself), one free-typed line "gift wrapping — $12", a drag to put wrapping last. Total assembles: $366. She leaves it as a draft overnight — tomorrow, [publish makes it official](#/M-04).
:::

*Every rule in TaroGrid — ready-made or hand-built — is the same three slots. Learn to read them and no automation is ever a black box.*

:::demo rule-anatomy · "Three slots" · 16s
Trigger: the event the rule watches → Check: only act if this is true → Then / Else: what happens, and what happens otherwise.
:::

## The three slots

:::cards
- **TRIGGER — when?** The event being watched: *status changes to Confirmed · an invoice becomes Overdue · a new row arrives · stock hits zero.* No trigger event, no rule run — rules react, they never roam.
- **CHECK — but only if? (optional)** A question that must be true before acting: *amount over $50? · fraud light is red? · assignee is empty?* Checks are what make one trigger serve many policies.
- **THEN / ELSE — do what?** The action: book, notify, set a field, send a reminder. **Else** covers the check failing — often "just tell me instead."
:::

## Reading a real one

*The commercial pattern:* **WHEN** delivery status becomes In transit *(trigger)*, **IF** the customs document field is empty *(check)*, **THEN** mark the order Urgent and notify Maya, **ELSE** mark it Approved. One card, an entire policy — readable by anyone in ten seconds.

:::why Why insist on this shape?
Because automation you can't read is automation you'll fear, and automation you fear stays off ([AU-01](#/AU-01)). Three fixed slots mean every rule answers the same three questions in the same places — your seventeenth rule reads as easily as your first.
:::

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Triggers watch *changes*, not states — "status becomes Confirmed" fires at the moment of change, not hourly while it stays Confirmed. (This is why nothing double-fires.)
- 💡 Can't decide between two actions? That's usually one rule with a Check, not two rules.

## Example

:::example
Maya wants reminders only for *serious* overdue invoices. Trigger: invoice becomes Overdue. Check: amount over $50. Then: remind the customer. Else: just notify Maya. Small invoices get her judgment; big ones get the robot's punctuality.
:::

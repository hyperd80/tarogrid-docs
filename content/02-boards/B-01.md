**Goal:** a board that fits your real process — built in five minutes, shaped to grow.

:::demo build-board · "Blank to working" · 14s
A blank board named for what a row will be → groups appear: stages you'd say out loud → columns: one fact each.
:::

## The steps

1. **Create the board** — in the **Work** category ([your canvas, C-05](#/C-05)). Name it for what one row *is*: "Deliveries," "Leads," "Content" — never "Maya's stuff."
2. **Add groups** — the colored sections. Use the stages you'd say to a teammate: *New / In progress / Done*. Two or three is plenty to start.
3. **Add columns** — one fact per column: a Status, a Person, a Date, a Number. Start with 4–6; add more when a real need shows up, not before.
4. **Add the first real row.** A board designed around real rows fits; a board designed around imagination doesn't.

## Shape rules that pay off later

- **One fact per column** — a "Details" mega-column can't be sorted, filtered, or watched by automations ([C-06](#/C-06)).
- **Groups are stages, columns are facts.** If you're making a group per customer, you want a Customer column instead.
- Reorder columns and groups anytime by dragging — shape is never final.

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Don't build ten columns for a future that may not come — empty columns teach your team to ignore columns.
- 💡 Copying a process from paper? Build the board *while doing the process once* — the real steps will disagree with your memory of them.

## Example

:::example
Maya's packing board: groups *To pack / Packed / Handed over*, columns *Order · Who packs · Deadline · Notes*. Built Tuesday in four minutes; Wednesday she added a *Fragile?* checkbox because real parcels asked for it. That's the rhythm — shape follows use.
:::

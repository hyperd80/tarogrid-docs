*The board is the data. A view is a pair of glasses. Swap glasses all day — the data never flinches.*

:::demo views-glasses · "Three pairs of glasses" · 16s
The same rows as a Table → as Kanban cards you drag between stages → on a Calendar, where anything with a date has a place.
:::

## The principle first

A **view** changes how rows are *shown*, never what they *are*. Switch from Table to Kanban and back — nothing was edited, nothing moved, nothing was lost. This is why you can experiment with views fearlessly.

## The three views today

:::cards
- **Table** — rows and columns, best for *editing many facts fast* ([mastery: B-04](#/B-04)).
- **Kanban** — cards in stage columns, best for *moving things through a process* ([B-05](#/B-05)).
- **Calendar** — items on dates, best for *anything scheduled*: deliveries, content, deadlines.
:::

A **Timeline/Gantt view** is on the roadmap 🔜. (And your [Sales Form](#/S-12) is a special fourth door — a view *outsiders* use to add rows.)

## Which glasses when

Ask what your eye is doing: **comparing facts** → Table. **Watching flow** → Kanban. **Watching time** → Calendar. Same board, all three, whenever — many teams live in Kanban and audit in Table.

:::why Why not one perfect view?
Because "what should I pack today?" and "which orders are stuck?" are different questions about the same rows. Views let each question have its ideal answer without duplicating the data — one truth, many angles ([the two-boards trap, C-02](#/C-02)).
:::

## Example

:::example
Maya's Orders Board, one day, three glasses: morning in **Kanban** (drag the confirmables), afternoon in **Table** (fix two addresses, sort by total), evening in **Calendar** (what's promised for tomorrow?). Zero copies of anything, three jobs done.
:::

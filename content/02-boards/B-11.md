*Fear is the enemy of learning. This article exists so you stop being careful in the wrong ways.*

:::demo undo-trash · "The safety net, in layers" · 14s
Deleted things go to Trash, not to heaven → restore brings them back, history and all → and a 500-row import is one undo step.
:::

## The layers, top to bottom

:::cards
- **Undo** — reverses your last edits, and understands *units of work*: a 500-row import undoes as **one step**, an [ASK cascade](#/A-04) sweeps back as one step. Ctrl/Cmd-Z, breathe.
- **Trash** — deletes are a move, not an execution. Items, rows, even boards sit in Trash, restorable with their history intact.
- **Cell history** — every cell remembers its past values, who and when ([C-06](#/C-06)). Even after an undo of an undo, the trail exists.
- **Honest confirms** — the few genuinely irreversible actions *say so, plainly, first*. If TaroGrid didn't warn you, it's recoverable.
:::

## What this means in practice

Try the weird filter. Reorganize the groups. Import the messy file. Every one of those is reversible — so the cost of experimenting is minutes, not data. Teams that know this learn the product twice as fast, because they push buttons instead of scheduling meetings about pushing buttons.

:::why Why so many layers?
Because "be careful" is terrible advice for software. Humans slip — the design question is whether a slip costs three seconds (undo) or an evening (no undo). TaroGrid chooses three seconds, everywhere it possibly can — and tells you honestly where it can't.
:::

## Pitfalls

- ⚠️ Trash isn't eternity — purged-after-a-while is the deal. Restore what matters when you notice, not next quarter.
- ⚠️ "Undo" undoes *your* edits — a teammate's changes are theirs to undo. (History shows whose was whose.)

## Example

:::example
Ben imports the wrong customer file — 500 junk rows across the Stakeholders view. Old world: an evening of cleanup. Here: **Undo import**, one step, gone. He re-imports the right file and mentions it at lunch as a joke instead of a confession.
:::
